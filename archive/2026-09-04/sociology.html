Context disadvantage and residential mobility from early life into adulthood: impacts on socio-economic outcomes

Sergi Vidal, Maël Lecoursonnais, Riccardo Valente, Maria Brandén

Full text
Individuals’ socio-economic trajectories are shaped by both persistence and change in their residential environments, making it essential to examine how the socio-spatial dynamics of context disadvantage and residential mobility interact over the life course. While prior research has often studied these factors separately and within specific life stages, less is known about their compound effects and their interrelatedness across childhood and adulthood. This study addresses this gap by investigating how exposure to residential contexts and residential mobility from birth influences socio-economic outcomes in early adulthood. Using Swedish longitudinal register data, we analyze residential trajectories and their association with economic and occupational (dis) advantage among individuals born between 1990 and 1993. Our findings show that mobility magnifies the associations between context exposure and socio-economic outcomes. Prolonged exposure to disadvantaged areas is most harmful when it coincides with moving, consistent with a compound penalty of instability and resource-poor environments. Second, moves in childhood are generally detrimental, while adult moves are opportunity-enhancing unless they occur in disadvantaged contexts, where the mobility premium levels off. Third, exiting disadvantaged contexts is associated with higher attainment than remaining persistently exposed, indicating that later socio-spatial trajectories can attenuate earlier disadvantage. These findings underscore the importance of considering both residential context and mobility across the life course for understanding—and interrupting—the reproduction of socio-economic inequality.

One overworking breadwinner or two earners? Family economic standard of living by breadwinner arrangement, 1960 to 2018–2022

Andrew Taeho Kim

Full text
Has the rise of rewards to long and inflexible work schedules altered the household-level economic viability of gendered divisions of paid work within marriage? This paper examines whether household arrangements centered on an overworking single breadwinner have become increasingly competitive, in terms of family economic standard of living, with non-overworking dual-breadwinner households. As prior research documents increasing rewards to overwork, particularly in professional and managerial occupations, overworking single-breadwinner families may have become economically competitive with dual-breadwinner households in which neither partner overworks. Using decennial Census and American Community Survey data from the Integrated Public Use Microdata Series covering 1960 to 2018–2022, we compare breadwinner arrangements across the income distribution and decompose changes in family income into changes in men’s earnings, women’s earnings, and non-labor income. Results show that overworking male- and female-breadwinner families experienced substantial relative gains at the top of the income distribution. Decomposition analyses indicate that these gains were driven primarily by accelerated earnings growth among overworking single breadwinners rather than earnings stagnation among dual earners. Although dual-breadwinner families with overworking breadwinners remain the most advantaged group, the gap between them and overworking single-breadwinner families narrowed substantially by 2018–2022. These findings suggest that labor market reward structures associated with long and inflexible work schedules may reshape family-level economic incentives in ways that reinforce gendered divisions of paid work, particularly among affluent families.

Politics & Society

Separating the Militants from the Moderates: Brazilian Industry and the Far-Right Bolsonarista Movement

Lucas Sorzano de Azambuja

Full text
Why do some capitalists in the Global South come to firmly support far-right movements while others do not? This article explores this question by examining divisions among Brazilian industry leaders over the right-wing government of Jair Bolsonaro, from 2019 to 2023. Drawing on fieldwork and interviews with leaders in Brazil's main industrial association, the Federation of Industries of the State of São Paulo (FIESP), this article argues that distinct relations to the state and varying degrees of economic precarity led FIESP leaders to either remain staunch supporters of Bolsonaro's government or grow critical. Leaders in medium-sized firms from shrinking domestic manufacturing sectors decried their exclusion from policymaking under previous administrations and became some of Bolsonaro's most ardent backers. In contrast, while initially supportive of Bolsonarismo , many leaders from more competitive, internationalized sectors dominated by large firms grew critical of Bolsonaro, particularly following his chaotic response to the COVID-19 pandemic. These moderate leaders recognized the structural power they leverage over state actors, while prioritizing gradual reform and a stable political environment. In this way, different relations to state actors led to a politics of moderation or militance. The article concludes by arguing how the political rationalities tying capital to the state shape intraclass cleavages and business’ response to emergent far-right movements.

European Sociological Review

Estimating the influence of learning disabilities on educational attainment: a sibling comparison analysis with a gender perspective

Jinho Kim, Keun Young Kwon

Full text
This study investigated the longitudinal relationship between learning disabilities (LD) in adolescence and educational attainment, as well as the underlying mechanisms behind this relationship. Using a sibling sample from the National Longitudinal Study of Adolescent to Adult Health (N = 1,710), this study employed sibling fixed effects models to account for unobserved family-level characteristics such as genes and early childhood family and social context. Sobel mediation analyses were performed on a gender-stratified sample to investigate the following mechanisms: educational aspirations and expectations, educational effort, and school-based relationships. Sibling fixed effects estimates revealed that LD in adolescence was negatively associated with years of schooling completed in adulthood (b = −0.494, P &lt; 0.01). Gender-stratified models showed that only the association for girls was statistically significant (b = −0.689, P &lt; 0.05). Among girls, this association was partially explained by a combination of educational aspirations and expectations, educational effort, and school-based relationships, with educational aspirations and expectations being the most salient mechanism. These findings highlight that LD is not only an individual cognitive designation but also a socially mediated experience with long-term educational consequences, especially for girls. Policymakers and practitioners may consider developing and implementing interventions that address both educational and social challenges faced by girls with LD.

Guiding ideas and constraints of administrative practice in the dispersal and accommodation of asylum seekers in Germany

Fabian Schmid

Full text
Germany’s quota system for the dispersal of refugees establishes a mandatory mechanism for refugees and subnational authorities to reduce conflict and uncertainty. The perception that this system is completely rigid is, however, misleading, as administrative implementation shows signs of flexibility. This paper provides empirical insights into how refugee dispersal and accommodation policies are shaped by guiding ideas of asylum administration as well as structural constraints. It draws on 12 expert interviews with officials involved in allocation and accommodation decisions across different levels in Bavaria and Saxony-Anhalt. Administrative practice is primarily guided by concerns of efficiency and cost-effective housing provision. In addition, practices of group differentiation reflect specific understandings of integration, aimed at maintaining control and avoiding situations perceived as detrimental to social integration.

Population, Space and Place

Places of Opportunity? The Role of Neighbourhood Socioeconomic Advantage on Upper Secondary Education Attainment Among Young Descendants of Immigrants in Denmark

Andreas Lindegaard Jakobsen, Anja Jørgensen

Full text
The integration of immigrants and their descendants has long been central to European and OECD policy agendas, with educational attainment considered a key indicator of integration success. Yet, persistent native‐immigrant gaps in educational attainment remain evident, including in the Nordic countries. Understanding how social determinants such as neighbourhood conditions may shape educational outcomes is therefore important. While neighbourhood‐effects research has traditionally mainly focused on the negative consequences of poverty and deprivation, growing attention has been directed towards the potential role of concentrated socioeconomic resources and neighbourhood advantage. However, relatively little research has examined these associations among descendants of immigrants. This study investigates the association between neighbourhood socioeconomic advantage during adolescence (ages 10–15) and upper secondary education attainment in emerging adulthood (ages 20–25) among descendants of immigrants in Denmark. Using longitudinal population register data on 26,697 individuals nested within 2701 neighbourhoods and analyzed using multilevel logistic regression, we examine whether adolescents living in more socioeconomically advantaged neighbourhoods are more likely to complete upper secondary education. The findings indicate that descendants of immigrants from the most socioeconomically advantaged neighbourhoods are more likely to complete upper secondary education than those from less advantaged neighbourhoods. The association is substantially attenuated but remained after adjustment for a wide range of individual, family and neighbourhood covariates. Overall, the findings suggest that neighbourhood socioeconomic resources may contribute to educational attainment among descendants of immigrants and should be considered alongside family and individual factors when addressing educational inequalities.

From Entry to Embeddedness: How Urban‐to‐Rural Newcomers Reshape Development Outcomes in Peri‐Urban China

Pingyang Liu

Full text
Rural digitalisation expands access to information and participatory channels, but greater digital provision does not necessarily produce equal opportunities to participate. Less is known about whether local governing arrangements compensate for digital disadvantage or instead increase the participatory returns to greater digital capability across different governance tasks. Using face‐to‐face survey data from 616 residents in 19 peri‐urban villages in Shanghai, China, this study examines institutional participation, voluntary community service and willingness to participate in village‐based economic development. Three differentiated patterns emerge. Intensive face‐to‐face governance attenuates the negative digital‐capability gradient in village‐level decision‐making, although the evidence remains suggestive; the same governance mode more clearly compensates for limited digital capability in voluntary community service. Most robustly, the regulatory influence of village rules strengthens the relationship between independent online information‐search capability and willingness to participate in local economic development. Cross‐domain tests reveal no comparable conditioning patterns. The findings show that governance arrangements that facilitate participation need not reduce participation inequality: their distributive consequences depend on the organisational requirements of the participation task. In Chinese village governance, digitalisation therefore operates through, rather than independently of, relational and informal institutional arrangements that shape who can convert digital resources into participation across different governance tasks.

Life‐Course Mobility and Occupational Trajectories: Gender Disparities in Later‐Life Economic Welfare Among Rural‐Hukou Adults in China

Lei Che, Jiali Lu, Tolulope Ariyo

Full text
China's rural‐to‐urban transformation has produced diverse mobility and occupational histories, but their implications for economic welfare in later life remain unclear. Using CHARLS life‐history data for 8840 middle‐aged and older adults with rural hukou, this study reconstructs annual mobility and occupational states from 2000 to 2014 and links the resulting trajectories to household per capita non‐food consumption in 2015. Sequence analysis identifies two mobility and four occupational trajectories. Survey‐weighted OLS models show that sustained predominantly intra‐provincial mobility is associated with higher consumption than stable no mobility, while managerial wage‐employment mixed and non‐agricultural self‐employment trajectories are associated with higher consumption than stable agricultural employment. These associations vary by gender. The sustained‐mobility contrast is weaker among women, whereas the non‐agricultural wage‐employment contrast is more positive. The decomposition identifies a significant male‐female consumption gap. Its marginally significant explained component accounts for 19.62% of the gap. Mobility trajectories account for a significant share of the gap, whereas the occupational‐trajectory contribution is small. Results are robust to excluding observations with low silhouette widths. Later‐life economic welfare is thus associated not with mobility or non‐agricultural employment in general, but with the particular pathways through which spatial and working lives unfold.

School Choice Regulation and Fertility: Evidence From China's Multi‐School Zoning Policy in Compulsory Education

Lei He, Qingfei Meng

Full text
School admission rules in compulsory education are a key determinant of both educational equity and household child‐rearing costs. China's multi‐school zoning (MSZ) policy is a core reform aimed at equalizing access to quality compulsory education. It replaces residence‐based school assignment with random allocation within expanded attendance districts and has been rolled out across provinces on a staggered schedule, yet its causal impact on household fertility decisions remains largely underexplored. Using individual‐level data from the 2010–2020 China Family Panel Studies and exploiting the staggered rollout of the policy with a difference‐in‐differences design, we estimate the causal effect of the MSZ policy on individual fertility. We find robust evidence that this education equalization policy significantly increases fertility, raising the individual probability of childbirth by approximately 2 percentage points. Mechanism tests identify two primary channels underlying the pro‐fertility effect, namely alleviation of housing cost burdens and reduction in expected long‐term intensity of educational competition. We further document substantial heterogeneity in policy effects across both individual and regional dimensions. At the individual level, the fertility‐promoting effect is attenuated among women aged over 35 and those with tertiary education. At the regional level, the pro‐fertility effect is significantly weaker in provinces with stronger clan cultures and lower pre‐policy housing prices.

Migration and Cities in Chile: Building Territories of Solidarity in Hostile Contexts

María Fernanda Stang, Carolina Stefoni, Aline Bravo, Víctor Félix Da Silva

Full text
This article analyzes practices identified at the territorial level in Antofagasta, a city in northern Chile. These practices have successfully established infrastructures of solidarity between the national and migrant populations, resulting in what we refer to as territories of solidarity. Following Schillinger’s work (2020), we argue that within these territories, experiences of solidarity emerge through contentious processes stemming from daily coexistence in a group of informal settlements (known as campamentos in Chile), mutual support efforts, the creation of alliances, and the production of (counter)spaces and (counter)knowledge. These practices of solidarity take on configurations linked to the organized social fabric in this territory and to the relationships forged with governmental, institutional, and organized actors at different levels. This experience not only emerges and coexists but also suffers and resists hostile migration policies, along with a hegemonic discourse that criminalizes and otherizes migrants.

The Supply of Demands: What Black Lives Matter Demanded in 2020

Arica Schuett

Full text
Despite the significant role social media played in mobilizing Black Lives Matter supporters, political science research on this topic focuses on the years before the massive mobilizations in 2020. This paper draws on a novel dataset of BLM chapters’ tweets during the George Floyd protests to examine the language activists used to facilitate protests during the movement’s peak public support. Despite having more experience mobilizing followers, older chapters were less likely to encourage followers to pursue change through conventional electoral politics even in the midst of a presidential election year. Instead, older chapters were more likely to share educational resources or media that cultivated a collective identity among supporters and followers.

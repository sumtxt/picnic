When Digital Exposure Meets Everyday Buying: Understanding Consumer Behaviour in FMCG

Rithik Bhattacharjee

Full text
Consumers today may discover a product on social media, encounter it repeatedly through digital advertising, and still make the final purchase from a neighbourhood store. This study explores the relationship between digital exposure and everyday consumer behaviour in the fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) sector. Using a consumer survey dataset containing 26 usable responses, the study examines purchase frequency, purchase channels, exposure to social-media promotions, reported influence of online advertising, customer satisfaction, and recommendation intention. The findings show that social-media exposure is relatively common, while reported influence of online advertising is more mixed. Traditional retail remains important, with kirana stores and supermarkets accounting for most reported purchase channels. Mean satisfaction was 3.92 out of 5 among respondents with valid satisfaction scores, while recommendation intention was predominantly positive. Exploratory statistical analysis did not identify a statistically significant association between online-ad influence and recommendation intention (χ² = 0.26, p = 0.61), nor a statistically significant relationship between satisfaction and recommendation intention (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.21, p ≈ 0.31). The findings suggest that digital visibility should not automatically be interpreted as behavioural influence. Consumer decisions appear to involve a broader combination of digital communication, product experience, purchasing convenience, availability, and customer service. Given the small and youth-concentrated sample, the study is exploratory and does not claim population-level generalizability. The paper highlights the need for larger and more diverse studies examining how digital engagement interacts with the wider FMCG consumer journey.

Human Circuits in Cold War Taiwan: RCA, Labour Governance, and the Developmental State

Ethan Chiu

Full text
Industrial development depends on institutions that organise technological learning and govern production. In Cold War Taiwan, these institutions also shaped who could challenge industrial harm. Drawing on field research, interviews, legal records, and institutional histories, this article argues that Taiwan’s developmental capacity was selective. A paired history of RCA Taiwan and the separate RCA-ITRI technology transfer project shows how investment promotion and labour governance privileged officially selected industrial objectives over workers’ independent authority. Public sponsorship equipped semiconductor learning with personnel, facilities, and foreign knowledge. Labour administration, managed representation, and corporate control of hazard information restricted workers’ demands for protection. Pay, housing, and production routines made exposure difficult to avoid, while repeated inspections failed to secure compliance. Missing records, withheld information, and corporate restructuring then obstructed compensation claims. Workers and their supporters challenged these barriers by assembling legal, scientific, and archival resources. Their struggle places labour protection within the history of developmental capacity, alongside the acquisition of technology.

Explaining historical persistence in Germany's electoral geography across 130 years: a cleavage-perspective on continuity in spatial patterns of party support

Leon Gawenda

Full text
This article studies the long-run persistence of electoral geographies. Drawing on cleavage theory, I argue that spatial patterns of party support persist where cleavages remain politically salient and the social groups they mobilise stay in place. I evidence this argument for Germany, combining electoral and census data from the German Empire (1890–1912) and reunified Germany (2002–2021) at the municipality, county and electoral-district level. The spatial analyses reveal substantial continuity in the electoral geographies of the mainstream left and right. The analysis traces both to one source, the persistent spatial distribution of Catholics. Christian democrats continued historical strongholds of the Center Party where Catholics stayed concentrated, while the SPD persists as its mirror image in the non-Catholic space. Persistence therefore rests on the stability of the religious geography and cleavage, not on the class cleavage and the economic geography, which has changed markedly. The findings position cleavage-based persistence as a complement to cultural-transmission accounts of historical legacies, locating continuity in the ongoing mobilisation of stable social groups rather than in transmitted attitudes alone.

Why Politicians Retweet: News Trustworthiness, Emotion, and Partisan Asymmetry in Elite News Sharing among US Congress Members

Apeksha Shetty, Esther Simmendinger, Jula Lühring, Annie Waldherr, David Garcia, Hannah Metzler

Full text
On social media, politicians' retweets can determine which claims gain visibility in online and in wider political debate, yet the factors associated with these retweeting decisions remain poorly understood. Combining natural language processing measures of emotional tone and partisan cues with politician-level characteristics, we analyze the retweeting behavior of United States Members of Congress between 2021 and 2023 using multilevel logistic regression models. Using all news-related retweets during this period, paired with a negative sample of non-retweets, we examine how tweet content and legislators' ideological extremity predict the amplification of tweets linking to more and less trustworthy news domains. We find that partisan identity is a stronger predictor of retweeting than other factors. While politicians generally preferred retweeting content with links to trustworthy sources, this preference weakened when tweets contained party-related references. Anger and fear predicted higher retweeting likelihood when tweets referenced political opponents, while anger predicted lower retweeting likelihood when tweets referenced political allies. Furthermore, ideology conditioned how politicians responded to news trustworthiness: among Republicans, increasing ideological extremity was associated with weaker preference for trustworthy sources, with the most ideologically-extreme Republicans being especially likely to retweet tweets with links to less trustworthy domains. Together, these results suggest that politicians use retweets to reinforce partisan boundaries between 'us' and 'them'. This identity-centered pattern to retweeting explains how information from less trustworthy sources gains traction in polarized digital environments.

Agreement Without Accuracy: Six Systematic Defects an LLM Annotation Study Could Not See in Its Own Reliability Statistics

Hon-Ren Lin

Full text
Researchers who use large language models as annotators are commonly asked to demonstrate reliability, and test–retest agreement under a fixed seed is the cheapest evidence. We report a study in which it was systematically misleading. Annotating 1,902 student UML class specifications under a fixed snapshot, seed and zero temperature, we compared three prompt configurations, each run twice. A three-stage pipeline appeared to raise answer-level agreement from .647 to .845, with chance-corrected agreement rising for every code and internal consistency satisfied throughout. Comparing per-code prevalence led us to inspect outputs; five systematic defects emerged: an instruction promising an input never supplied (wrong on 64.7% of its flags at κ = .971); a stage receiving element names stripped of the types it was to judge; residual error after repair; enumerated conditions firing outside their scope; and alignment tables stored without half of what they summarised. A sixth lay in our checking procedure: the audit rule we wrote agreed with a blind reading on only 67.5% of cases, and the twenty cases read to confirm it were drawn from its own output. A blind reading of forty random submissions, judged before any output was seen, is the study's only anchored estimate; under it our one reported repair cannot be shown to help. None was surfaced by a reliability statistic; those that altered the model's inputs or rules raised agreement by easing its task. Reliability from repeated runs should be accompanied by prevalence comparisons and a sample read blind, drawn independently of the detector.

Cultural Cognitive Scaffolding

Helena Miton

Full text
In many domains, human life has undergone dramatic changes—globalized exchanges, complex technological systems, large societies—that would both require and result from rapid cognitive change. In this work, I introduce the Cultural Cognitive Scaffolding Hypothesis, which posits that (1) human cognition is uniquely culturally evolvable because our cognitive processes rely on environmental resources that are largely culturally evolvable, and, as a consequence, (2) it is these specific cultural innovations, called cultural cognitive scaffolds (CCS), that are responsible for much of the rapid, recent changes in human cognition. Importantly, to achieve this effect, these cultural innovations must balance cognitive impact and cultural success. The historical expansion of exchange networks offers an ideal case to illustrate how diverse cultural innovations helped manage the high uncertainty associated with exchanges across larger physical and cultural distances, and a window into how cultural success can be achieved for different cultural innovations. Finally, this framework has important implications for our understanding of the uniquely open-ended character of human culture.

From Affective Experience to Mechanism: A Critical Realist Case Study Architecture Using Reflexive Thematic Analysis

Varun S. Nair

Full text
Studying affective life qualitatively poses a known problem. Burnout, emotional labour, and slow shifts in teacher–class relations are real with measurable effects, yet not directly visible; we infer them from behaviour and language. Interpretation always mediates. How can we claim mechanisms without pretending to observe them? This paper proposes a case-study architecture using reflexive thematic analysis within a critical realist frame. It integrates five usually separate elements: a multi-perspectival case design; abduction and retroduction as genuinely different inferences; systematic assessment of rival explanations; a procedure for sorting context from mechanism when they do not separate cleanly; and a hierarchy of claims calibrated to evidence. The combination yields what neither purely interpretive nor purely causal work can: fallible mechanism explanations that take meaning and causation equally seriously. The argument is specified, not demonstrated. Two computational simulations provide proof of concept. The first distinguishes two known ground-truth mechanisms across 40 synthetic cases, with 80% overall recovery (95% CI [68%, 92%]; 85% and 75% per mechanism). The second adds a third rival—perceptual reconstruction, in which teacher behaviour is unchanged but students report change—and distinguishes all three across 60 synthetic cases, with 95% overall recovery (100%, 100%, 85%). The three-way test uses tighter noise parameters than the two-way, so rates are not directly comparable. Both test only one component and neither substitutes for empirical validation on real classroom data, which remains future work.

Ideology on the U.S. District Courts: Reexamining Presidential and Senatorial Appointment Power

Nicholas O. Howard, David A Hughes, Philip Warncke

Full text
Unlike U.S. appellate courts, scholars of the American federal judiciary typically lack ideological estimates for district court judges that are derived directly from their behavior. Rather, scholars have primarily relied upon proxy measures based upon the preferences of a judge's appointing president or those of home-state senators in the event senatorial courtesy is in effect. Nevertheless, senatorial courtesy is a norm, and norms can be broken. It remains an open question whether home-state senators genuinely constrain the president when it comes to the ideological tenor of appointees to the district courts. We produce novel ideal point estimates for 1,680 U.S.~district court judges appointed between 1913 and 2009 based upon their votes in approximately 100,000 cases, in addition to contextual factors unique to judges and the process by which they were appointed. We map these ideal points into the Judicial Common Space and make them publicly available to American courts scholars. These ideal points differ meaningfully compared to previous proxy measures and improve predictive power in judge voting behavior. Importantly, despite a battery of statistical tests, we find that senatorial courtesy appears to play a minimal role with respect to the revealed preferences of U.S. district court judges compared to the role the president's ideology plays. If senatorial courtesy indeed constrains presidents in the separation of powers game, it is likely on some other, non-ideological dimension.

The Substitution Trap: Development Aid and the Composition of Government Spending in Indian States

Krishna Vadlamannati, Roberto Iacono

Full text
The question of whether aid supplements or substitutes government spending has long been debated in public finance literature. Building on Vadlamannati and Iacono (2026), who examine how development aid affects discretionary fiscal transfers to Indian states, we address a distinct fiscal question: how does aid exposure affect the composition of subnational government expenditure? Using panel data for 30 Indian states over 1979-2019 (41 years) and an instrumental variable strategy addressing potential endogeneity, we find that greater exposure to aid is associated with a lower share of capital expenditure and a higher share of revenue expenditure. This pattern is consistent with substitution of externally aided projects for domestically financed spending. The effect however varies systematically with electoral incentives. In states with greater electoral competition, aid is more strongly associated with capital expenditure, suggesting that electoral incentives can encourage state governments to channel external resources towards visible capital investments. Our findings extend the evidence on fiscal consequences of aid in India from the allocation of intergovernmental discretionary transfers to the composition of state government expenditure. Our findings reveal that the developmental effects of aid depend not only on how resources reach subnational governments but also on how governments subsequently allocate them.

Disaffording the Pedestrian: When Affordances Are Removed

orla fahey

Full text
Designing for pedestrian road safety is challenging. Designs need to work for a broad and diverse range of pedestrians, while also considering the needs of other vulnerable road users, such as cyclists. Pedestrians are ranked as the most vulnerable in the road user hierarchy and designing for those with protected characteristics is critically important for road safety designers. One key challenge faced is designing the environment so that safety provisions can align with national and international goals to encourage people to walk or wheel more for health, economic and environmental reasons. Doctoral research was undertaken to understand whether and how design can improve pedestrian road safety and used key study sites in London to investigate the relationship between safety affordances in public space and pedestrian safety. One main finding was that often, during a redesign, safety features are removed. The consequences of these changes make it difficult for pedestrians to maintain their safety, particularly those with protected characteristics. Beyond potential physical harm, there are psychosocial impacts: people may avoid a route if they feel it is unsafe or leads to conflict in contested spaces which leaves pedestrians feeling unable to navigate a space confidently. These experiences are not routinely captured within road safety data, which focuses largely on KSI (killed or seriously injured) outcomes. I have coined this phenomenon of removing an affordance a disaffordance. The concept comes from over five years of doctoral research into pedestrian road safety in the UK, which focused on pedestrian-only study sites in London that had been redesigned as shared-space zones. Identifying safety affordances and disaffordances in the built environment helps to identify opportunities for broader and more systematic pedestrian participation in the design process. The research also developed GroundMap, a diagnostic approach for identifying and mapping affordances and disaffordances and anticipating the potential consequences of their removal. At a broader level, the term disaffordance might be extended to identify other environments where the removal of an affordance has significant consequences. While this research applies the concept specifically to pedestrian safety, its wider application across other environments and domains is identified here as a direction for future work.

Nirmal Gurung

Full text

The paper concludes that Nepal’s debt is sustainable in the near term. However, the increasing vulnerabilities in the form of currency depreciation, inadequate revenue mobilization efforts and low efficiency utilization of the borrowed funds should be responded to in order to address long-term sustainability.

Rounding and the bill-size gradient in tipping

Toby Qin, Vrund Gurjar

Full text
Restaurant tip percentages often decline as bills increase. Rounding is one proposed explanation because a fixed upward adjustment adds more to the tip percentage on a small bill. We examine how much of the observed decline can be explained by adjustments of up to one dollar. We analyzed 22,446 checks with positive recorded tips, no automatic gratuity and bills of $10–$200 from three North Carolina restaurant outlets. For checks with a whole-dollar tip or reconstructed final payment, we allowed the pre-rounding tip to fall anywhere from one dollar below the recorded amount to the recorded amount, with a minimum of zero. We calculated the smallest and largest bill-size coefficients across these possible amounts, controlling for outlet, month, guest count, opening hour and weekday. Bootstrap checks resampled dates, weeks and servers. Each scheme used 999 draws, with the rounding bounds recalculated for each resampled dataset. Tip percentages fell by 4.29 points per bill doubling. Even the adjustments that most reduce this decline leave a coefficient of −1.62 points, which remains negative across all three bootstrap checks. Five-dollar rounding or restricting bills to $30–$200 allows the fitted decline to disappear. Upward dollar rounding cannot fully explain the negative gradient in the primary sample. Larger rounding increments and the exclusion of small bills produce ranges containing zero.

Sustaining Imbalance: International Financial Foundations of Growth Models

Benjamin Braun, Lucio Baccaro, Joshua Cova

Full text
Current account imbalances, which have surged again in recent years, are key drivers of global economic instability and conflict. Political economy explanations tend to focus on wage restraint and currency undervaluation. However, in a world of unrestricted cross-border capital mobility, the binding constraint for sustaining imbalances over time is financial flows. To address this gap, we develop a theoretical framework linking current account imbalances, growth models, and domestic financialization. We hypothesize that deep and liquid financial markets enable the reproduction of consumption-led growth by relaxing the current account constraint, while financial underdevelopment favors export-led growth by incentivizing capital exports.

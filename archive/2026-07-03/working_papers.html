The Fear of Forgetting: The Reasons & Methods By Which Bhutanese-Nepali Refugees Work to Preserve Their Cultural Identities

Aryaa Rajouria

Full text
The Hindu, Nepali speaking Southern Bhutanese have always been seen as being dissimilar to the rest of the Buddhist, Dzongkha speaking population. At the start of the 20th century, however, dissimilarity turned into discrimination. The Southern Bhutanese people with Nepali descent were given a choice. They could either absolutely disengage with their own culture and agree to acclimate to the Buddhist ways, or they were exterminated. A majority of the population decided to continue partake in their own culture, and were thus, violently expelled from the nation. As a result of an exclusionary sovereign who sought to create an ethnically united nation, the Southern Bhutanese fled the country. Since then, this population who identify themselves as the Bhutanese-Nepalese have migrated several times: first to refugee camps in Eastern Nepal, where they believed their ancestors once dwelled, or nearby locations in India and then they were scattered all over the world, particularly places like United States, Canada, and Australia. While the Bhutanese-Nepalese refugee population in Massachusetts has inhabited the area for almost a decade how, the members of the older female generation have yet to assimilate. Linguistic barriers and divergent lifestyles cause the Bhutanese Nepalese population to fold within their own. Based on a snowball-sampled focus group, I demonstrate that the women of the Bhutanese-Nepalese community preserve their distinct cultural identity through the practice of religion and by utilization of their language as a means by which to validate their survival after histories of multiple migrations and expulsion. By doing so, these women are creating a Bhutanese-Nepalese nation.

Poverty and cardiovascular diseases: Individual and neighbourhood effects using a registry-based study in Czechia

Josef Bernard, Klára Benešová, Martin Bobák, Dagmar Dzurova, Klára Hulíková Tesárková, Lukáš Kahoun, Pavlína Netrdová (Charles University)

Full text
The study investigated the relationship between poverty, place, and cardiovascular health in a Central and Eastern European country with a high burden of cardiovascular disease (CVD). We examined the effects of individual and neighbourhood poverty on CVD. We analysed data linked across several national registers covering 5.1 million working-age residents of Czechia (2019–2024), combining individual health and socioeconomic records with neighbourhood poverty measures. Individual poverty and neighbourhood poverty were assessed by receipt of subsistence-level poverty social benefits. The outcome was defined as CVD-related hospitalisation and/or mortality (ICD-10 I00–I99). Associations of CVD with individual and neighbourhood poverty were analysed using logistic regression models while accounting for residential sorting. Individual poverty was strongly associated with CVD-related hospitalisation and mortality, with individuals experiencing poverty exhibiting more than twice the odds of CVD. Living in a high-poverty neighbourhood was also associated with increased CVD risk, although the contextual effect was substantially smaller than the individual-level effect. The findings demonstrate that both individual and neighbourhood poverty independently shape cardiovascular health. While individual socioeconomic disadvantage is the dominant predictor of CVD risk, neighbourhood poverty exerts a smaller contextual influence. Incorporating place-based measures into health research can improve the identification of socially vulnerable populations and advance understanding of how local socioeconomic environments contribute to health inequalities.

Unseen, Unheard, Undervalued: Relational Mechanisms of Gender Inequality in the Dutch IT Sector

Elise Jantine van der Mark

Full text
Despite decades of policy progress, workplace gender equality in the Netherlands has stalled. Male-dominated sectors such as IT remain among the most unequal and exclusionary for women. Addressing these inequalities requires attention to women’s lived experiences, yet empirical research on Dutch women in IT remains strikingly limited. Drawing on feminist relational theory and the Gender@Work framework, this paper develops a relational diagnostic approach. It examines how gender inequality is (re)produced through everyday workplace interactions. The empirical basis consists of in-depth interviews (n=27) and three participatory group sessions (n=33). Adopting a participatory action research design, the study centers women's own accounts and collective analysis of their workplace experiences. The data reveal a dense web of interactions and relations involving colleagues, managers, clients, and institutional actors. Within these interactions, inequality is enacted through patterned relational practices rather than isolated incidents. Microaggressions, epistemic exclusion, and everyday interactional dynamics operate across contexts to contest women’s legitimacy, regulate their voice, and mark their embodied presence as deviant from the masculine norm. The analysis identifies seven recurring relational mechanisms through which gender inequality is systematically reproduced across interconnected domains. This extends Acker's concept of gendered organizations and offers a diagnostic framework with concrete entry points for organizational intervention.

Police legitimacy in Bangladesh: a review of trust, accountability, and democratic governance

Ashfaq Ferdous, Sayed Osman Goni Rafat

Full text
This study reviews and synthesises the literature on police legitimacy in Bangladesh. Drawing on 55 sources published between 1979 and 2026, it examines how legitimacy has been conceptualised, the factors that shape it, and the implications of recent accountability and reform debates. The findings show that police legitimacy in Bangladesh cannot be understood through procedural justice alone but is shaped by the interaction of citizen experiences, institutional performance, accountability arrangements, community engagement, and democratic governance. The literature identifies six interconnected themes: conceptualisations of legitimacy, trust and procedural justice, institutional sources of legitimacy, accountability deficits, community engagement, and democratic policing and reform. It also reveals a shift from concerns with trust and procedural fairness towards greater attention to accountability, democratic oversight, reform, and legitimacy restoration, particularly in the post-2024 context. The study develops a multi-level framework integrating interactional, institutional, and democratic dimensions of legitimacy and identifies priorities for future research.

Attrition Bias in AI-Administered Interviews

Mikkel Stahlschmidt, Tobias Priesholm Gårdhus, Hjalmar Bang Carlsen

Full text
Who finishes an AI-administered interview, and how does that group differ from who finishes a standard survey? We report a pre-registered four-arm randomized experiment (N = 1,591) assigning Danish online-panel adults to a standardized survey, a standardized chat, an adaptive AI chat, and an extended adaptive AI chat, all covering same substantive content. Completion falls monotonically from 91.8% to 66.7% across arms. The chat interface drives most of the gap (a 14.6 percentage-point drop), limited adaptive probing adds almost nothing (2.3 percentage-point), and extended probing leads to exhaustion and attrition (8.2 percentage-point). Dropout is socially selective: in the most demanding arm, respondents aged 18 to 34 are more likely to complete than those aged 56 and older, and completers are more educated and more likely to use a computer rather than a smartphone. AI-interview samples are therefore younger, more educated, and more desktop-oriented, with direct consequences for inference of results.

Paz Total? How Ceasefires Backfire

Juan F. Vargas, Daniel Mejia, Andrés F. Rivera

Full text
Ceasefires are often designed to reduce violence while facilitating peace negotiations or humanitarian access. But poorly designed truces can backfire. This paper examines the 2023 ceasefires decreed by Colombia’s government with several organized criminal groups simultaneously under the Paz Total (Total Peace) policy. Using difference-in-differences on a municipality-month panel, we find that while more visible and salient forms of violence such as homicides, terrorist attacks and massacres were unaffected, less visible forms of violence against civilians, such as extortion, forced recruitment of minors, and threats, increased substantially in municipalities with ceasefire group presence. These patterns are consistent with strategic substitution: armed groups shifted from more visible to less visible forms of violence when political constraints changed. We formalize this mechanism in a structural model of strategic violence allocation, calibrate its parameters to the reduced-form estimates, and simulate policy counterfactuals. The calibration implies that roughly a 50% increase in the detectability of less visible forms of violence, through independent verification missions or community reporting systems, would be needed to fully offset the ceasefire’s perverse effects, restoring total violence to approximately its pre-ceasefire level. Our findings highlight the unintended consequences of inadequately designed ceasefire agreements and underscore the need for credible monitoring and enforcement mechanisms.

Occupational Safety and Health Management in Very Small Enterprises: A Scoping Review Protocol

Marion Poirier, Jessica Dubé, Anouk Aubert-Simard, Benedicte Nauche, Caroline Jolly

Full text
Very small enterprises (VSEs), defined as organizations with 1 to 19 employees, represent 88.4% of Canadian businesses. Despite their economic weight, their occupational safety and health (OSH) practices remain poorly documented, and the literature focuses mainly on larger enterprises. This scoping review aims to identify and map OSH activities in VSEs across OECD countries, including prevention practices, implementation strategies, and organizational factors, in a context where limited material, financial, and human resources often pose challenges for OSH management. Guided by the Arksey and O’Malley framework and the JBI approach, MEDLINE, Embase, PsycINFO, and Scopus will be systematically searched for English- or French-language publications from 2014 to 2024. The search strategies will be developed by a specialized librarian (BN) and will be described in detail in the supplementary material 1. Records will be independently screened by two reviewers (ergonomists AAS and MP), and data will be extracted by the full research team (AAS, MP, CJ, and JD) using a structured charting form. Extracted information will include study characteristics; OSH topics (e.g., workplace safety, injury or accident prevention, risk assessment, training and information, health promotion); the person responsible (e.g., worker, owner-manager, or external stakeholder); and reported facilitators and barriers to integrating these OSH strategies. Descriptive mapping and thematic synthesis will be conducted, with study management supported by Covidence® and thematic charting supported by NVivo 15®. Findings will be disseminated through submission to the interdisciplinary journal WORK: A Journal of Prevention, Assessment & Rehabilitation and through presentations at scientific and professional events, in support of a broader six-project research program on OSH management in Québec VSEs (Dubé et al., 2024). By highlighting OSH strategies used by VSE owner-managers and workers, this scoping review will support researchers, practitioners, and policy stakeholders seeking feasible, context-sensitive approaches to improve OSH in micro-enterprises and very small businesses.

Carry-over effects in the within-subject spectator task

Linda Dezső, Antal Ertl, Valeriia Chukaeva, Gergely Hajdu

Full text
The spectator task elicits third parties’ inequality acceptance, allowing researchers to estimate population distributions of fairness-preference types. In its within-subject form, spectators make two subsequent choices in situations involving merit- and luck-based inequalities with randomized order. We document a carry-over effect: redistribution is lower in the second choice, and inferred fairness-type distributions vary with choice order. We cannot determine whether this pattern is driven by a psychological mechanism active during repeated choices or a contrast between inequality causes. Either interpretation implies a violation of causal transience and calls for caution when using this design.

An ESCO-Based Skill-Gap Detection Framework for SMEs: A Design Science Prototype of an Intelligent Learning Management System

Angelo Leogrande, Mauro Di Molfetta, Nicola Magaletti, Valeria Nortarnicola, Maria Giovanna Trotta

Full text
The growing misalignment between workforce competences and the skill requirements of digitally evolving occupations is a critical barrier to SME competitiveness in the Industry 4.0 and 5.0 transitions. This study develops and demonstrates, through a working prototype, an ESCO-aligned analytical framework for skill-gap detection and adaptive reskilling, produced within the LUCE (LUtech Campus Ecosystem) project. Its contribution is theoretical, methodological, and technological rather than purely applicative. Drawing on human capital theory, the knowledge-based view of the firm, and skill-biased technical change, it conceptualizes reskilling as the relaxation of a firm-level human-capital–technology complementarity constraint, using ESCO to render this constraint observable and commensurable across firms. Methodologically, it defines the skill gap as a standardized, ontology-grounded measure; technologically, it integrates functions usually kept separate — performance analytics, skill assessment, and learning provision — into a single pipeline, instantiated as a proof-of-concept Intelligent Learning Management System. Workforce competences are extracted from anonymized employee CVs via a deterministic, rule-based Natural Language Processing procedure, mapped to ESCO preferred labels, alternative labels, and concept URIs, and linked to occupational requirements through occupation–skill relations. A Skill Gap Indicator, defined as the complement of evidenced ESCO competence coverage, yields a mean of 0.956, interpreted as a conservative upper-bound estimate rather than a literal measure of absent competences. The prototype then translates detected gaps into targeted training recommendations. As a research prototype, the system is validated along its technical and analytical dimensions rather than for training effectiveness; the relationship between gap closure and firm performance is advanced as a proposition for future longitudinal evaluation.

Angel Versus Devil: Ethical and Unethical Leadership Case Study Comparison

Chen JianYuan, Hao Puyan

Full text
Leadership significantly influences organizational performance, ethical governance, and sustainable development. This study compares two contrasting historical leaders, Charles Ponzi and Robert Owen, to examine the impacts of unethical and ethical leadership on corporate social responsibility (CSR) and the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Using a comparative case study approach based on historical evidence and business ethics literature, the research analyzes their leadership styles, decision-making processes, and long-term social consequences. The findings indicate that Ponzi's deceptive and fraudulent leadership generated short-term financial gains but ultimately destroyed stakeholder trust, caused substantial economic losses, and undermined sustainable development. In contrast, Owen's servant and ethical leadership promoted employee welfare, education, social equity, and responsible management, demonstrating that ethical leadership can create lasting organizational and societal value. The study further discusses how ethical decision-making, transparency, and corporate governance can reduce unethical behavior and strengthen CSR practices. This research concludes that although unethical leadership may produce temporary success, ethical leadership provides stronger foundations for organizational sustainability, stakeholder confidence, and long-term competitive advantage. The findings offer practical implications for business leaders, policymakers, and management educators seeking to promote responsible leadership and sustainable organizational development.

Opera decolonized - Listening to Afghan Women’s Voices

Kasia Głowicka

Full text
Opera is a form where music, staging, dramaturgy, costume, and space are co- equal — a total sensory experience that demands full presence from its audience. This paper investigates whether that formal quality can be turned against opera's own colonial tendencies. Working within The Airport Society collective, I examine how the opera-installation Unknown, I Live With You (2018), setting clandestine writings by Afghan women, transforms opera's European form into a respectful artistic space for non-European voices. I argue that interlocking formal decisions together refuse the patronizing frameworks that historically position non-European subjects as exotic victims requiring Western salvation. The non-hierarchical structure of the work extends to the collective process that produced it: the same democratic principle governing our artistic decisions shapes what the audience experiences. The result is cross-cultural listening that treats Afghan women's socio-political reflections with intellectual gravity rather than humanitarian condescension.

Andrés Gabriel Varas Quijón

Full text
n mayo de 2026, Garfield AI —el primer despacho de servicios jurídicos del Reino Unido autorizado y regulado por la Solicitors Regulation Authority para operar principalmente mediante inteligencia artificial— obtuvo, ante el Tribunal del Condado de Wandsworth, la primera victoria de un «despacho de IA» en un juicio contradictorio frente a una parte asistida por abogados tradicionales. Este trabajo sostiene que el dato relevante no es la sustitución del abogado por una máquina, sino la inversión funcional de roles: la IA produce los documentos y el jurista deviene intérprete y responsable de lo generado. A partir del caso se examina el modelo regulatorio británico (autorizar a la firma, no a la máquina), se lo contrasta con la matriz estadounidense (Regla 5.4 del Modelo ABA, doctrina del ejercicio no autorizado del Derecho y sandboxes de Utah y Arizona) y europea (Reglamento (UE) 2024/1689 y Carta Ética de la CEPEJ), y se evalúa su trasladabilidad al ordenamiento chileno. Se concluye que el obstáculo chileno no reside en la propiedad de la firma —la colegiatura es voluntaria desde 1981 y no rige una prohibición equivalente a la Regla 5.4— sino en el monopolio personal de la postulación (Ley N° 18.120, arts. 1 y 2), reforzado por sanción penal (art. 3) y por el carácter intuitu personae del título de abogado (arts. 521 y 527 del Código Orgánico de Tribunales). La ficción de la «firma-IA» no es trasplantable; sí lo es, en cambio, su función legítima: la inteligencia artificial como herramienta de preparación bajo patrocinio, firma y responsabilidad de un abogado habilitado. https://zenodo.org/records/20848233

The Measure Makes the Finding: Racial disparity, democracy, and punishment in the U.S. states

Miura Meng

Full text
This paper asks whether American states with healthier democracies treat Black and white residents more equally in their prisons, and finds that the answer is mostly an artifact of how the gap is measured. The usual measure, the Black/White imprisonment ratio, runs higher in more democratic states. Taken at face value, that says democratic health and racial fairness in punishment work against each other. But the ratio is higher there only because those states imprison white residents so rarely, not because they imprison Black residents more. By the absolute Black rate, those states are no worse, even a little better. The paper carries the same question through three further choices an analyst barely notices, comparing states against each other rather than against their own past, sorting them into trajectory types, and reading Florida's 2018 felon re-enfranchisement by its text against its turnout. Each choice moves the answer. A single Bayesian model of the underlying imprisonment rates then derives the competing measures as contrasts of one posterior, so their disagreement is reported as a probability, not a row of conflicting numbers. In a field that quantifies racial injustice with ratios and gaps, the measure of disparity is often the finding.

Learning interdependence: youth arts as relational skills in Singapore

Rimi Khan

Full text
This article examines how arts-based co-curricular activities (CCAs) in Singaporean higher education navigate the tension between state pragmatism and student experience. Situated within the SkillsFuture framework and a meritocratic ethos that instrumentalises education for economic productivity, arts programmes can be viewed as sites for acquiring ‘21st-century skills’. The Breadth and Depth of Opposition to Antiracism: How Resistance to Antiracist Claims and Activism Shapes White Americans’ Political Attitudes

Rachel Wetts, Robb Willer

Full text
Previous scholarship has most often examined White Americans’ opposition to antiracist claims, policies, and movements as a dependent variable, shaped by forces such as racial prejudice and Whites’ group interests. However, little research has examined the consequences of these attitudes, despite recent events suggesting the importance of these views in conservative mobilization. Across two surveys and three experiments, we explore how opposition to antiracism (or anti-antiracism) shapes the political views of White Americans (total N=6,495). We find anti-antiracism has broad endorsement and effects: it is prevalent among Whites and is a powerful predictor of many policy positions, including being more strongly related to support for Donald Trump than several measures of racial prejudice or political ideology. At the same time, anti-antiracism is especially strongly related with Trump-related attitudes among religious, conservative, White Republicans (Studies 1a-b). Next, across two pre-registered survey-experiments (Studies 2a-b), we find anti-antiracism predicts Whites’ responses to policy frames and political candidates that invoke issues of race. Finally, we find opposition to antiracism is resistant to counterevidence: a factual intervention correcting misperceptions of anti-Black hiring discrimination influenced perceived rates of discrimination, but did not influence more general anti-antiracist attitudes (Study 3). These findings suggest opposition to antiracist claims and activism is a powerful coalitional resource for the Republican Party, allowing them to draw broad-based support while activating an even stronger response from their base, via attitudes that persist in the face of counter-evidence.

The Constituency for Personal Politicians: Social Embeddedness and Voter Behavior in Indonesia

Harry Janos Dienes, Burhanuddin Muhtadi, Hui Yuan Neo, Eitan Paul

Full text
Do voters support personal intervention by politicians to facilitate access to public services? Does contingency – the defining characteristic of clientelism – matter? We conduct a survey experiment to test Indonesian voter reactions to three political service delivery strategies: impartial bureaucratic monitoring, personal intervention for all constituents, and clientelistic intervention for supporters only. Personal intervention is punished compared to programmatic monitoring, and contingency generates no further electoral costs than non-partisan personal intervention. Yet through a candidate survey, we find elected candidates make deals for access to services more often than unelected ones. To reconcile these findings, we show that social embeddedness moderates reactions. Voters with low social density and those who have approached politicians or bureaucrats for help find personal or clientelistic strategies as appealing as programmatic. These groups constitute a (minority) constituency for personal intervention as a legitimate electoral strategy. The results emphasize social relationships in voter reactions to clientelism.

Lame Duck by Primary: Effects of Electoral Incentives on U.S. House Representatives

Kisoo Kim

Full text
How do electoral incentives motivate representatives to exert effort and shape policy positions? While reelection is widely recognized as representatives' primary motivation in democratic politics, accurately estimating its effects is challenging because representatives self-select into rerunning or retiring decisions. Leveraging legislators’ lame duck status after primary elections, this article estimates how electoral incentives affect U.S. House Representatives. The Village Framework: A Maternal-Centered Model for Social Support, Clinical Care, and Structural Equity Across the Reproductive Continuum

Beatriz Chagas Amarante

Full text
The cultural proverb "it takes a village to raise a child" has shaped collective understandings of social support for decades. Yet a fundamental omission persists within this construct: the mother herself is rarely positioned as a primary beneficiary of the village she is expected to build. This paper presents The Village Framework, a maternal-centered conceptual framework proposing that the health and wellbeing of mothers across the reproductive continuum depend on the stability and interaction of three interdependent pillars: Social Support, Clinical Care, and Structural Support. These pillars rest on a Cultural and Equity Foundation that functions as the operative lens through which each pillar must be evaluated and delivered. Drawing on Bronfenbrenner's ecological systems theory, the Social Ecological Model, the Convoy Model of Social Support, the Fourth Trimester Framework, and the Reproductive Justice Framework, this paper synthesizes previously separate strands of theory into a single maternal-centered architecture. The framework advances three core propositions: first, that the mother must be recentered as a primary unit of care within the village construct; second, that the village is structurally unstable when any single pillar fails, regardless of the strength of the others; and third, that the period from preconception through the first year of the child's life is a foundational developmental window for both maternal and child health. A complete village is hypothesized to reduce maternal mortality risk, improve continuity of care, reduce postpartum mental health burden, strengthen mother-infant bonding, decrease perinatal substance use disorders, and create healthier developmental conditions for children. Four testable hypotheses are proposed to guide future empirical work. The framework is universal in scope and equity-conscious in application, with particular relevance to populations for whom systemic pillar failures have been most persistent.

Purpose – This paper proposes a methodological framework that combines Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR) survey with GIS-based spatial analysis to strengthen non-invasive documentation and risk assessment of archaeological sites managed under United States Cultural Resource Management (CRM) law. TECHNOLOGICAL VARIATION IN IRON SMELTING IN SOUTHWESTERN TANZANIA: AN INTEGRATED XRF AND XRD ANALYSIS OF MALUNGU AND KATUKUTU FURNACES

John Uche Ngonadi, Chioma Vivian Ngonadi, Abiodun Olayinka Ganiyu, Micheal C Atogu

Full text
Iron smelting was an important technological practice in precolonial southwestern Tanzania, where metallurgical production was organized through two structurally distinct furnace traditions: the tall natural-draft malungu furnaces of the Fipa and the shorter globular katukutu furnaces of the Nyiha in Isenzanya. Although the architectural characteristics of these traditions have been well documented, comparatively little archaeometric research has examined whether their typological differences are reflected in the composition of their production residues. This paper reports integrated X-ray fluorescence (XRF) and X-ray diffraction (XRD) analyses of slag and furnace wall fragments from Miangarua (a malungu site in Sumbawanga) and Isenzanya (a katukutu site in Mbozi District). The slags from both sites are dominated by fayalite and a glassy silicate matrix and exhibit broadly comparable silica and alumina contents. However, Miangarua slag is enriched in MgO, K₂O, CaO, and P₂O₅, suggesting a greater contribution from fuel ash, whereas Isenzanya slag retains higher Fe₂O₃ concentrations, indicating less complete iron reduction or the use of richer ore feeds. The mineralogical and geochemical characteristics of the residues suggest that malungu and katukutu furnaces shared a common iron-silicate-based bloomery tradition, with differences reflecting variations in operational scale and smelting intensity rather than fundamentally distinct metallurgical recipes. Interpreted within the broader technological landscape of southwestern Tanzania, the findings provide a baseline for future archaeometallurgical investigations of technological variation and knowledge transmission in the Corridor region. Keywords: Archaeometallurgy; Iron smelting; Bloomery slag; XRF; XRD; Southwestern Tanzania

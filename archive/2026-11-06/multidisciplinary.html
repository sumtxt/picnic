Full text
Children learn to understand and speak from the speech around them, yet how that everyday input shapes learning remains unresolved. Large language models show that language is learnable from data, but their scale and training conditions differ sharply from a child’s natural experience. We trained cognitively feasible, child-specific models on longitudinal home recordings from eight children across their first 1000 days of life. Distributed range adaptation in human parietal encoding of numbers

Arthur Prat-Carrabin, Gilles de Hollander, Saurabh Bedi, Samuel J. Gershman, Christian C. Ruff

Full text
The brain’s representations are encoded in the collective activity of neural populations whose non-monotonic tuning properties define the population code. Adaptive behavior calls for flexible retuning of this code as stimulus statistics change across contexts. Yet how such adaptation occurs is unknown. Using fMRI during a numerosity-estimation task with variable number ranges, we show that number representations in human parietal cortex dynamically recalibrate to context, enabling context-sensitive behavioral accuracy. The tuning properties of neural populations collectively shift and rescale with the range. This distributed range adaptation achieves efficient coding in real time: neural precision scales with the range and predicts corresponding changes in behavioral precision. Individuals with stronger neural adaptation show larger behavioral adjustments. These findings extend static sensory efficient coding to adaptive representations of abstract magnitudes. Such flexible population tuning may constitute a canonical mechanism of encoding networks that enables the brain to sustain precise, adaptive behavior.

The Fireside Database: Large-scale evidence from 25,419 psychedelic support interactions

Felix Schoeller, Marc Santolini, Dana Xiadani, Mickael Eskinazi, Ashwin Murali, David Esselman, Wyatt Houtz, Wesley Bellanca, Joshua White

Full text
Most psychedelic experiences occur outside clinical settings, yet as policy frameworks rapidly expand to accommodate nonclinical use, no systematic data exist on who seeks psychedelic support, under what circumstances, or what makes that support effective. We present the Fireside Database, a US-based dataset comprising 25,419 peer support interactions conducted by 220 trained volunteers at Fireside Project’s Psychedelic Support Line between April 2021 and February 2026. The released resource integrates structured interaction logs with volunteer-coded metadata and 2777 caller satisfaction surveys across five outcome dimensions. Psilocybin was the most commonly reported substance (59.9% of coded interactions), while cannabis produced the highest distress rate (47.2%). The nested data structure—with 50 supporters each completing ≥10 surveyed interactions—supports multilevel modeling of individual supporter effects on caller outcomes. This resource enables research in psychedelic epidemiology, evidence-based training design, and the investigation of what makes psychedelic psychological support effective.

KOTAX: An integrated corporate disclosure corpus linked to a financial and tax panel of Korean listed firms

Sejin Myung, Hyungjong Na, Junil Seo, Minsoo Lim, Hwa Su Hong, Hyungjoon Kim

Full text
The financial condition and business environment of a firm are difficult to fully capture using only the quantitative figures presented in financial statements. Business reports and audit reports contain important information that complements structured financial data—such as business structure, risk factors, accounting policies, management judgments, and audit opinions—in the form of text and tables. However, existing financial and tax panels and corporate disclosure document corpora are generally provided separately, and publicly available data resources that directly link quantitative data with original disclosure documents at the same firm–year level remain limited. Moreover, procedures for collecting disclosure documents, identifying firms, matching fiscal years, cleaning XML, restoring table structures, and converting text vary across researchers, which may undermine the reproducibility of analyses and the comparability of findings across studies. To mitigate these problems, this study constructs KOTAX, an integrated corporate disclosure corpus linked to a financial and tax panel of Korean listed firms. KOTAX consists of 25,145 firm–year observations for 2,259 firms listed on KOSPI and KOSDAQ from 2011 to 2025, together with 60,908 linked documents—business reports, separate audit reports, and consolidated audit reports—covering 2,256 of these firms. The firm–year panel extends the construction methodology of our prior dataset, KoTaP, to fiscal year 2025; the newly added elements are two tax-payment volatility measures (TaxPaid_Sale_SD3/SD5) and the firm–year-level linkage to original disclosure documents. Each document is linked to a panel observation by firm identifier, fiscal year, and report type, and we provide deterministic processing code that generates cleaned XML, structure-preserving text, and hierarchical JSON from the original XML. This enables researchers to combine quantitative financial and tax information with unstructured disclosure information at the same unit of analysis, and to conduct research on text analysis, financial figure extraction, table understanding, structure-based retrieval, and retrieval-augmented generation in a reproducible manner. Although its empirical coverage is Korean, the resource is not tax-specific: it can support research in financial reporting, auditing, disclosure, corporate governance, financial natural language processing, table understanding, and document retrieval, and it provides a non-U.S. benchmark for testing the portability of methods developed on other filing systems. The deterministic linkage and processing framework can also be adapted to other jurisdictions with jurisdiction-specific collection and parsing rules, supporting reproducible cross-market comparative research.

The resurgence of symbolic environmental management through imprinting effects

Yuki Torida, Hitoshi Mitsuhashi, Keisuke Ogihara, Akitsu Oe

Full text
Organizations often engage in symbolic adaptation—surface-level responses that sustain legitimacy under institutional pressures. Prior research views such actions as present-focused, yet corporate leaders are also embedded in historical contexts where earlier events shape their cognition and decision-making. Despite this alternative mechanism, little is known about symbolic adaptation as a historical product. Drawing upon imprinting theory, we argue that individuals exposed to predecessors’ symbolic adaptation during their formative careers retain it as a dormant repertoire that resurfaces when they later assume corporate leadership roles. We test this prediction using data on Japanese corporate leaders active in the 2000s–2010s who entered the workforce in the 1960s, when firms symbolically addressed rising environmental demands. We find that leaders imprinted by symbolic adaptation in the 1960s are more likely to engage in symbolic environmental management, although this effect weakens under the oversight of non-executive directors and the intervention of activist shareholders.

Absorbing shocks by accumulating risk: do financially stressed households take on underinsurance risk to manage liquidity constraint?

Antonia Settle, Maxim Ananyev

Full text
This study asks if shocks, such as job loss or illness, impact households’ insurance coverage and the role of cashflow constraint therein. As such, we explore changing dynamics of household risk where new climate risks interact with financial risks associated with the large and illiquid balance sheets that are typical of debt-laden and ‘financialized’ households. Using Australian panel data, we show that households reduce insurance coverage to reduce costs following a shock, with the largest cuts to coverage by households who have the least disposable income leftover after meeting debt servicing and other committed expenditure. We find evidence of a substitution effect between committed expenditure and insurance coverage. The study demonstrates an important dynamic of risk accumulation arising from financial stress and makes a key contribution to the insurance literature by revealing a significant new driver of underinsurance. Moreover, by presenting a vivid dynamic of household shock absorption through the accumulation of new risk, the analysis contributes to debates about how risks are shared between households and markets as household balance sheets expand.

Global value chains and the rate of incarceration in rich democracies

Matthew C Mahutga, Manjing Gao, Sharon S Oselin

Full text
In this article, we explain increases in incarceration among rich democracies with the expansion of global value chains (GVCs) through trade and its interaction with national labor market institutions. GVCs increase incarceration rates through both “supply side” and “demand side” channels. Southern manufacturing imports embody asymmetrical bargaining arrangements between leading firms and their suppliers in the global South. They therefore disproportionately impact the economic fortunes of lower-skilled segments of society that populate carceral systems, and disembed these segments from the postwar social contract. The worldwide expansion of GVCs exacerbates these effects, as the growing number of capable Southern suppliers worsens the exchange conditions governing leading and supplying firms over time. However, domestic labor market institutions ameliorate this dynamic by limiting both the supply- and demand-side channels through which GVCs promote incarceration. Pooled cross-sectional time-series regression analyses of 22 rich democracies over the 54-year period from 1963 to 2017 are largely consistent with our intervention. Southern imports increase rates of incarceration, and these effects are stronger as GVCs entrench worldwide. GVCs have weaker effects in countries with high union density and strong wage coordination. However, counterfactual histories suggest that GVCs are more important for observed increases in incarceration on average.

Markets embedded in publics: information-processing social systems under the attention economy

Tuukka Ylä-Anttila

Full text
This article theorizes financial markets and democratic publics as collective information-processing systems embedded in shared communicative infrastructures, such that markets can even be said to be embedded in publics. Bringing the efficient market hypothesis into dialogue with the liberal marketplace of ideas and the Habermasian public sphere, I identify four epistemic conditions these traditions depend on: diversity, independence, aggregation, and correction. I argue that price discovery is epistemically embedded in the same media environment through which public opinions form. The attention economy of engagement-driven media brings this into view by degrading that environment: it privileges affective arousal over informational quality, correlates judgements, and suppresses correction, in effect creating corrupted marketplaces of ideas. The comparison also shows a specific difference between the domains: market correction through arbitrage operates through market transactions as well as media communication, whereas counterspeech and persuasion in publics are more reliant on media, arguably rendering publics more vulnerable to media impairment.

Journal of Refugee Studies

Labour-market strategies and migration aspirations under legal uncertainty: Ukrainian refugees in Norway

Celine Borge, Joachim Vogt Isaksen

Full text
Drawing on twenty-three qualitative interviews, this study examines how labour-market strategies among highly educated Ukrainian refugees in Norway are shaped by migration aspirations under temporary protection. Existing research has largely treated labour-market integration and migration aspirations as separate phenomena, paying limited attention to how future aspirations influence refugees’ labour-market strategies. Building on theories of migration aspirations, social anchoring, and perceived opportunity structures, the analysis identifies three distinct labour-market strategies associated with different aspirations for settlement, return, and onward migration. The findings show that employment is understood as a means of securing legal stability, preserving professional identity, or developing resources for future mobility. Moreover, labour-market strategies are also shaped by how refugees perceive available opportunities through interactions with institutions, employers, and social networks. The article contributes to debates on refugee adaptation by showing how future orientations and perceived opportunity structures interact to shape labour-market navigation under conditions of legal uncertainty.

Not Just Money or Jobs: How Family and Policy Shape Heterogeneous Configurations of Urban‐to‐Rural Talent Migration Decisions?

Hang Liu, Guosheng Deng, Bingling Wei

Full text
Attracting and retaining skilled individuals remains a central challenge for rural development. This study examines how push, pull, and capability conditions combine to shape urban‐to‐rural talent migration decisions in China. Drawing on a survey of 1551 respondents across six provinces in eastern, central and western China, the study applies fuzzy‐set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) to examine the configurational effects of urban quality‐of‐life pressures, family care obligations, rural development prospects, PA, hometown attachment and self‐efficacy. The findings show that no single condition is necessary for an urban‐to‐rural migration decision. Instead, six sufficient configurations are grouped into four broader types: family‐care‐centred configurations, an external‐attraction configuration, a need‐fulfilment configuration and a capability‐enabled attraction configuration. Family care obligations and PA are prominent but configuration‐dependent conditions, whereas urban quality‐of‐life pressures operate primarily as a peripheral contextual condition. By integrating push, pull and capability perspectives within a configurational framework, this study advances a talent‐centred account of counter‐urbanisation. The findings suggest that rural talent policies should move beyond uniform incentives and address heterogeneous combinations of family needs, perceived opportunities, place‐based ties and individual capabilities.

Boomerang Young Adults in Seoul, South Korea: Residential Pathways, Health and Social Attitudes

Gum‐Ryeong Park, Jiawei Wu

Full text
Leaving the parental home is a key transition to adulthood. However, recent sociodemographic changes have produced more diverse residential pathways such as delayed home‐leaving or returning to the parental home (‘boomerang movers’). While prior research has focused on the characteristics and determinants of these patterns, few studies have examined how various residential pathways are related to trajectories of health and social attitudes in Korea. We used longitudinal data from the annual Seoul Young Adult Panel Study (2021–2024) of individuals aged 18–34 interviewed ≥ 2 waves ( n = 4895) and identified 4 residential pathways: persistent coresidence, persistent independence, boomerang and transition into independence. Outcomes were self‐rated health, suicidal ideation, social outlook (expectations regarding Korea's future relative to the present) and perceived social fairness. Mixed‐effects models were then estimated to examine differences in levels and rates across pathways. Compared with persistent coresidence, persistent independence was associated with a lower probability of reporting suicidal ideation at baseline and slower decreases over time. Boomerang adults had lower self‐rated health and higher suicidal ideation at baseline. However, they showed greater improvements over time: faster gains in self‐rated health, a more rapid decline in the likelihood of reporting suicidal ideation, larger increases in perceived social fairness and a less pronounced decline in social outlook. No significant differences were observed for the transition into independence group. Overall, residential pathways are linked to distinct trajectories of health and social attitudes during emerging adulthood. Boomerang living reflects initial disadvantages, gradual improvements and adaptation among urban young adults.

Crossing Boundaries in Myanmar: Strategies of Boundary‐Making vis‐à‐vis Nativist Citizenship Imaginary

Eduardo Sánchez Molano

Full text
This article examines popular narratives in Myanmar that use physical characteristics, such as skin colour, to argue that certain minority groups are non‐native and originally from the Indian subcontinent, and how effectively the military junta has enforced this view. Using Andreas Wimmer's (2013) typology of boundary‐making strategies, this research examines the experiences of unofficial Muslim minorities in the townships of Mingala Taungnyunt and Tamwe, Yangon, before the 2021 military coup, as well as early coup accounts, to discuss alleged discriminatory rhetoric by the Burmese State in determining who qualifies as part of the population it governs. The analysis is situated within the Citizenship Law of 1982, which created distinct categories of citizenship, and the supposedly privileged position of the Bamar people within the official list of 135 national races, tracing incentives for both closure and strategic inclusion. A first‐glance examination of discourses from above may suggest that physical traits are used to restrict access to resources and affirm group identities, with extreme categorisation increasing reliance on markers of distinction. Yet a deeper analysis from below reveals that association and discrimination extend beyond racial or ethnic prejudice, with many individuals unaware of their friends' and relatives' ethnic backgrounds. I argue that this apparent contradiction reflects not inconsistent enforcement, but strategic ambiguity: the state's classificatory apparatus is intentionally loose, and this looseness leaves room for individuals to negotiate, contest and reshape the boundaries it imposes. This research demonstrates how individuals engage with, challenge and reshape these processes, even as local governments manipulate citizenship membership.

Healing at Home or Re‐Marginalisation? Perceived Neighbourhood Environment, Sense of Belonging and Mental Health of Return Migrants in Peri‐Urban China

Lingling Su, Enyu Chang

Full text
Amidst China's transition into a new phase of urbanisation, the mental health of return migrants has emerged as a critical issue. Whether returning to rural communities offers the spiritual solace of ‘healing at home’ or precipitates a descent into ‘re‐marginalisation’ remains poorly understood, particularly in metropolitan peri‐urban villages where remembered hometowns are being reshaped by urbanisation. Drawing on the survey of return migrants in peri‐urban Zhengzhou, this study constructs and validates a moderated mediation model based on field survey data to systematically investigate the mechanisms underlying the impact of neighbourhood environmental perception on the mental health of returnees. The findings reveal that: (1) more favourable perceptions of both built and social neighbourhood environments were associated with fewer mental health problems, and perceived built environment was positively associated with perceived social environment; (2) multi‐scalar sense of belonging played a mediating role in the associations between perceived neighbourhood environments and mental health, with village belonging representing the more prominent mediating pathway and highlighting the role of emotional identification in return migrants' post‐return adaptation; and (3) migration experience moderated the association between village belonging and mental health: longer out‐migration duration attenuated the protective association of village belonging with mental health, whereas a higher host‐city tier and greater perceived social exclusion strengthened its negative association with mental health problems. This study uncovers the spatial‐emotional interactive mechanisms of psychological adaptation among return migrants, providing a robust theoretical basis and practical pathways for enhancing the mental health of returnee populations through the improvement of human settlements and community governance.

Care Preferences Beyond Filial Piety: How Second‐Generation Chinese Migrants in the Netherlands Plan Care for Ageing Parents?

Peiyao Zhang, Özge Bilgili

Full text
As more first‐generation Chinese migrants in the Netherlands reach older age, increased attention is required to reconcile their care needs and expectations with the expectations and perspectives of their adult children. Moving beyond filial piety, this study examines how second‐generation Chinese migrants develop and reflect on their preferences and plans for their parents' care. The study employs a developmental life course perspective (DLCP) to contribute to the understanding of the evolution of care planning from an individual perspective. Drawing on semi‐structured interviews, the findings show that adult children are generally willing to support their parents, but seek simultaneously to establish boundaries in response to parental expectations. Institutional care is often preferred over family‐based care, particularly when intensive support is anticipated. In this regard, filial piety is neither fully nor seamlessly transmitted across generations. The analysis based on DLCP sheds light on multiple tensions that are either intensified or mitigated through the perceived relevance of Dutch and Chinese cultures, past and present parent–child relationships, sibling dynamics, and agency in striving toward ideal care arrangements.

‘People Recognise Us as the Bravest of the Brave’: Retired Gurkha Soldiers Negotiating Employment, Racialisation and Identity in Britain

Shovita Dhakal Adhikari, Louise Ryan

Full text
This article aims to contribute new insights into ageing migrants and masculinity by drawing upon life story interviews with retired Nepali Gurkha soldiers settled in South‐East England. Less is known about middle‐aged and older migrants who migrate not to retire per se but to pursue a second or third career in later life. Our research adds new insights into how these men mobilise capitals to navigate new and unfamiliar job markets, post‐retirement, in their middle and older age. Drawing upon the concept of ‘presentation of self’, the paper illustrates how Gurkha men utilise their martial identity—as brave, strong and loyal ex‐soldiers in the British Army—to challenge discrimination and navigate new lives in Britain. However, the Gurkha image is also based on colonial racialisation. Nonetheless, we find that preserving their Gurkha identity and their legacy of service to the British Crown was not only to gain recognition of their military service and unique status, but also to assert their masculine identities in the new contexts in Britain.

Rank, Degrading Treatment, and the Italy‐Albania Agreement

Jamal Barnes, Obed Adonteng‐Kissi

Full text
In November 2023, Italy signed a five‐year agreement with Albania to transfer asylum seekers intercepted in international waters by Italian vessels to Italian‐run detention centres in Albania for asylum processing and deportation. The agreement has been controversial and raises several human rights issues. This article examines these human rights concerns and argues that the agreement is degrading by design. Analysing policy documents, legal cases, speeches, international human rights and refugee law, and philosophical and legal scholarship on the concept of degrading treatment, this article argues that the cumulative effect of subjecting people to unnecessary suffering, discrimination, and dehumanisation creates a structural risk of degrading human dignity that brings asylum seekers down in rank. This article makes an important contribution to the policy debates over the externalisation of asylum seekers in Europe by shedding light on the link between policy designs and the risk of degrading treatment.

Journal of Race, Ethnicity and Politics

Right-Wing Shift? Evaluating Authoritarianism, Social Dominance, and System Justification Among People of Color Before and After the 2024 Election

Emily Ortiz, Ramona Alhambra, Andrew M. Engelhardt, Efrén Pérez

Full text
Non-trivial shares of people of color (PoC) have long identified as Republicans, and many of them helped cement Donald Trump’s 2024 re-election. How durable was this shift toward the political right among some PoC? We test two claims in this brief research note. First, consistent with polarization literature, we claim that partisanship primed three conservative postures among Republican PoC during the 2024 campaign: authoritarianism, social dominance orientation, and system justification. Second, aligning with work on expressive survey responses, we argue that these postures faded significantly once Trump’s agenda produced visibly harmful consequences for PoC (e.g., deportation raids; weakened civil rights). Leveraging a three-wave panel of Black, Latino, and Asian adults ( N = 3,626), our pre-registered analyses indicate Republican partisanship in June 2024 heightened authoritarianism, social dominance, and system justification among Black, Latino, and Asian adults in December 2024. These patterns were fleeting, with partisanship’s impact on these postures withering by July 2025. We find scattered evidence that these postures intensified PoC’s Republican partisanship during this period, implying our results reflect campaign effects rather than an unerring endorsement of these right-wing inclinations among Republican PoC.

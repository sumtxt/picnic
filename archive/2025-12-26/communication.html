Media, Culture & Society

Tradwives, it’s not (leftist) feminism you dislike, it’s capitalism! Rereading Harry Braverman alongside the discombobulated politics of social media tradwives
Meaghan Furlano
Full text
This article examines what I call the discombobulated politics of the tradwife, a popular social media aesthetic embraced by women who champion “traditional” Western gender norms, the nuclear family, and pronatalism. Tradwives have become formidable cultural figures due to their vast audiences, ties to far-right politics, and ability to mobilize around widely shared emotions. I understand their politics as discombobulated insofar as they advance a false cause for a social world that can be more accurately explained through established social scientific frameworks, such as those advanced by Harry Braverman in his 1974 book Labour and Monopoly Capitalism . In this article, I identify recurring tradwife critiques of “feminism” and use Braverman’s analysis to rebut these misframings, redirecting attention to the true structural driver of discontent: monopoly capitalism. By disentangling tradwives’ animus toward “feminism” and relocating it within the dynamics of capital (or a “feminism” that is really capitalism in disguise), this article provides a more precise account of the forces contributing to the contemporary anti-feminist backlash. It concludes that while tradwives often identify real social problems, the emancipatory impetus that would otherwise steer them toward left-wing politics is subverted by the malevolent actors of what Naomi Klein calls the Mirror World.
Podcasting Islam in the Anglosphere: Muslim counterpublics in the UK, US and Australia
Shaheen Amid Whyte
Full text
Podcasts are emerging as a powerful medium for information exchange and public dialogue. Within the Muslim digital space, podcasts have become viable alternatives to mainstream media, where Muslims have been routinely marginalised, vilified and misrepresented. Drawing on counterpublics theory, this study employs digital ethnography to explore how Muslim-owned media platforms reclaim self-agency and construct online publics in Anglophone contexts. It examines the audience metrics and discourses of four popular Islamic podcasts: The Thinking Muslim (UK), Blogging Theology (UK), OnePath Network (Australia) and Zaytuna College (US), with a focus on their content production, charismatic appeal and pan-Islamic orientation. It finds that the podcasts actively shape counterpublics through their engagement with decolonial thought, traditionalist scholarship and the amplification of Muslim lived experiences. While this paper highlights the benefits of fostering Muslim representation and counter discourses, it shows how podcasts can equally be susceptible to insider bias, ideological silos and the rise of influencer culture. In particular, the paper outlines the unexpected crossover between left and right-wing counterpublics and new ways of constructing religious authority online.

Building a Permanent Filter

To filter journals, append the URL parameters hide and show and bookmark the resulting URL. For example by appending ?hide=APSR to the paper-picnic.com/politics, all articles from the American Political Science Review are hidden. To filter out content from multiple journals, use a comma separated list, i.e., ?hide=APSR,AJPS,JOP. To only show content from specific journals, use the show parameter, e.g., ?show=APSR,AJPS,JOP. The parameter hide and show can also include bundle tags for journals, e.g., ?hide=@IR will hide all international relations journals.

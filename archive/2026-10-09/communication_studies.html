Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication

The sequential replicant effect in interpersonal sincerity: evidence from AI-mediated apology and gratitude

Bolin Cao, Zhenming Li

Full text
Sincerity, a core interpersonal value that mobilizes emotional resources, faces distinct challenges in AI-mediated interpersonal contexts. Extending the Replicant Effect from informational trust to interpersonal sincerity, this study examines how AIMC awareness and AI involvement cues shape sincerity judgments and subsequent relational responses. Drawing on two pre-registered, two-round studies in apology (N = 546) and gratitude (N = 561) scenarios, we found that AI involvement cues reliably reduced perceived sincerity. Primed AIMC awareness showed a more sequential influence on later no-cue evaluations through AIMC suspicion. High-ambiguity AI cues produced sequential effects only in the gratitude context. These findings suggest that hybrid human-AI authorship may reshape how interpersonal sincerity is inferred and validated, while also showing that the effects of AI cues and AIMC awareness depend on cue clarity and relational context.

Social Media + Society

Seeing Is Believing? Most People Struggle to Detect AI-Generated Images

Mads Fuglsang Hove, Frederik Hjorth, Yevgeniy Golovchenko, Rebecca Adler-Nissen, Anja Bechmann, Arjen van Dalen, Sara B. Hobolt, Claes de Vreese

Full text
Generative artificial intelligence (AI) dramatically lowers the costs of creating high-quality, realistic images, allowing a wider range of actors to create and distribute such content on, for instance, social media. A core concern is that easy-to-use generative AI tools can be misused for manipulation through misinformation and microtargeted persuasive material. However, we still need more contextual nuance to a fundamental question: To what degree can people discern photorealistic AI-generated images from authentic images? In a preregistered survey experiment, we find that, on average, people are not substantially better than chance at discerning AI-generated images from authentic images, regardless of the context of the image. While younger people, highly analytical-thinking individuals, and experienced users of generative AI have better discernment abilities, no demographic group other than young individuals (<25) performs substantially better than chance. Contrary to our expectation, we do not find that people’s confidence in their discernment abilities varies depending on the context of the image. As generative AI technology likely advances and the realism of AI-generated images continues to improve, these findings are concerning, as people already struggle to discern between AI-generated and authentic images, even in an experiment where they have been primed and are assessing images of relatively low quality.

Algorithmic Literacy and Quotidian Curation Practices: Rethinking User Agency in the Social Media Regulation Era

Agata Stepnik, Joanne Gray

Full text
This paper examines the practices ordinary social media users employ to influence the content served to them by platform algorithms, and the relationship between these practices and levels of algorithmic literacy. Practices were documented across TikTok, Facebook, Instagram, Twitter (now X), YouTube and Reddit during 65 sessions, conducted as part of a digital ethnographic study of Australian social media users aged 18–30. The paper presents a taxonomy of curation practices, categorised as active or passive, and inviting or dissuading of content. The diversity of practices used by participants in the study points to a degree of user agency, exercised within proprietary algorithmic content systems, not previously explored in the literature. In the context of rising public concern about the harms of social media use, particularly to young people, and policy responses that focus on restricting access to social media, this paper offers a more nuanced account of user experiences online, foregrounding their agency, practical expertise and capacity to deploy protective strategies when engaging with algorithmically mediated content flows.

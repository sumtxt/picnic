Positioning enjoyment and appreciation as key components of news use

Jennifer Hoewe, Brett Sherrick, Diane Jackson, Sam Woodland

Full text
In response to the continued convergence and hybridization of news and entertainment media content, this research explores the roles of enjoyment and appreciation in contemporary news use. Three studies (two surveys and one experiment) show a clear link between enjoyment, appreciation, and news use. Both enjoyment and appreciation are positively related to agreement, value alignment, attitude change, and attitude reinforcement in the context of news use. News enjoyment is related to increased positive and decreased negative emotions, while news appreciation is connected to an increase in and tolerance of mixed emotions. Importantly, news enjoyment decreases actual knowledge while facilitating greater affective polarization. News appreciation increases more positive feelings toward other people. These results illustrate the value of merging research on entertainment and news media, and they also suggest that news enjoyment is a driving factor in the growth of less democratic news use outcomes.

Academic freedom: defending a public good

Thomas Hanitzsch

Full text
This essay, an expanded version of a Presidential Address delivered at the 2026 conference of the International Communication Association (ICA), addresses the global erosion of academic freedom and its implications for communication scholarship. Drawing on a survey of 546 ICA members, the article documents substantial restrictions on academic freedom, alongside widespread occupational intimidation. Most survey respondents have personally experienced hostility or intimidation related to their work, most commonly through social media and personal insults. Nearly half of them report altering their academic practice—e.g., by adjusting their teaching content and research topics or speaking out less publicly. While most attacks originate from the public and political actors, the survey shows that a significant amount of intimidation originates from colleagues and other academics. The essay argues that these findings demand a coordinated collective response, including institutional advocacy, solidarity networks, and recognition of academic freedom as an indispensable human right.

Social Media Platform Repertoires in Everyday Life: Measuring Social Media Use With Passive Metering

Moreno Mancosu, Mario Quaranta, Fabio Torreggiani

Full text
Social media platforms structure the ways in which billions of citizens access information. Nevertheless, to assess social media time use, most research relies on limited data sources, such as self-reported survey measures or behavioral data from single platforms or convenience samples. Using 6 weeks of passive metering of smartphone and computer activity from a quota sample of Italian adults (18–70, n = 1,457), this study provides a behaviorally measured account of platform-specific social media use across the adult population. We quantify time allocation across Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and Twitter/X and examine how use varies by sociodemographic factors. Results show that Facebook and YouTube dominate everyday social media time, accounting for nearly three-quarters of consumption, while Instagram attracts comparable average time from a narrower user base, and TikTok shows concentrated usage among younger adopters. Twitter/X occupies a marginal niche. Use is overwhelmingly app-based (>90%), a pattern that browser-based measurement cannot capture. Age is the strongest demographic predictor: Gen Z concentrates attention on Instagram and TikTok, while older cohorts favor Facebook; YouTube shows weak demographic patterning. Gender and education are consistent secondary predictors: women devote more time to Facebook, Instagram, and social media overall, and higher education is associated with lower aggregate use and a smaller share on Facebook and TikTok. Employment and income show null effects. Together, these behavioral, cross-platform figures provide a more accurate basis for weighting platforms by their actual audiences than self-reported or single-platform evidence allows.

Guardians of the cyberverse: Dispositional, attitudinal, and autobiographical correlates of user engagement against misogynist digital hate

Rinat Meerson, Kevin Koban, Jörg Matthes

Full text
As digital misogyny remains widespread, user intervention is an essential complement to lenient platform governance. Yet, willingness to intervene varies considerably across individuals and cultural contexts. In a cross-national survey conducted in Austria ( n = 791), France ( n = 800), Hungary ( n = 775), and Sweden ( n = 785), we examine how dispositional, attitudinal, and autobiographical characteristics relate to users’ direct and indirect intervention and support for moderation. Empathic anger consistently emerged as the strongest positive predictor of all three outcomes across countries. Perspective-taking, online self-presentation, and victimization experiences showed meaningful positive associations with intervention, while misogynist attitudes were inconsistently related. Toxic disinhibition showed a dual pattern, negatively associated with moderation support but positively associated with direct intervention across all countries, suggesting its potential to be cleverly repurposed toward counterspeech. These findings underscore the importance of emotionally activating literacy programs and highlight the need for culturally sensitive intervention models.

The managed sisterhood: Gendered labor and intimacy in Chinese fashion influencer industry

Qiheng Lin, Chenyu Dong

Full text
As China’s leading multi-channel network (MCN) institutions become increasingly industrialized and bureaucratic, their operations are paradoxically characterized by intensified emotional dynamics. Drawing on ethnographic research within a women-dominated fashion influencer team, this study identifies a sisterhood-based production model that embeds sisterly ties within hierarchical organizational structures. It traces the formation and collapse of this managed sisterhood. By cultivating an illusion of unity and belonging, intimacy makes labor more governable, obscuring coercion while facilitating the mobilization of emotional, aesthetic, and physical capacities. In this way, sisterhood becomes integral to production, intensifying labor and rendering precarity routine. Under platform capitalism, traditional Chinese sisterhood, once grounded in kinship and flexible resistance, is reconfigured into an occupational form of informal labor control, producing “disposable kinship” in which affective bonds become temporary and replaceable. This study challenges dominant political readings of sisterhood and demonstrates how intimacy itself becomes a mechanism of governance within platform capitalism.

Two Paths of Conversation: How Stories and Statistics are Associated With Community Engagement in Online Debate Surrounding Vaccine Science Skeptical Content

Han Li, Inhwan Bae, Pengfei Zhao, Natalie Bazarova, Drew Margolin

Full text
Statistical and narrative evidence represent two distinct ways of substantiating claims in public reasoning, but does this distinction matter for how people engage with vaccine science skeptical (VSS) content and the discursive spaces surrounding it? This study addresses this question through a large-scale, LLM-assisted observational analysis of online engagement with VSS posts and comments that support or counter such content using statistical versus narrative evidence in the Reddit community r/DebateVaccines . Building on Fisher’s distinction between rational and narrative paradigms , we theorize two paths of conversation through which evidentiary forms are associated with audience engagement with contentious vaccine discourses in a naturalistic social media setting: a rational pathway, marked by concentrated participation and corrective contestation around statistical claims and counterclaims, tending toward a single evidence-based truth “approved” by one-click reactions; and a narrative pathway, characterized by generative engagement featuring broader and more distributed participation, more discursive and generative engagement, and greater acceptance of competing viewpoints. These findings provide a springboard for theorizing evidentiary form as a key epistemic logic associated with how conversations evolve in real-world engagement with contentious, socially consequential issues such as vaccine misinformation, while informing hybrid approaches to addressing skepticism toward established vaccine science.

Constantly Connected, Differently Affected: Response Speed Shapes Parents’ and Adolescents’ Feelings of Connection

Razieh Pourafshari, Miriam Brinberg, Joseph B. Bayer

Full text
Response speed in digital communication serves as a signal of availability, yet past research has overlooked how speed expectations in specific relationships translate into social connection. This study addresses this oversight by integrating theoretical perspectives on constant connectivity and chronemics to test the implications of perceived response speed for parent-adolescent relationships. Using a 28-day dyadic daily diary design of 142 parent-adolescent dyads, we examined how one’s own and one’s partner’s perceived response speed were associated with feelings of connection. Results revealed asymmetric and interdependent patterns. Faster response speed perceptions predicted greater connection, but these associations were more pronounced for parents. Additionally, parents’ perceptions of adolescents’ response speed predicted adolescents’ feelings of connection. These findings indicate that response speed perceptions can act as a mechanism that links connectivity to social connection in everyday life, but this depends on relational role.

Pupil Responses in Healthy Aging: Task Demands Shape Age-Related Differences

Eszter Ágnes Kiss, Gábor Gesztesi, Peter Pajkossy

Full text
The locus coeruleus-noradrenerg (LC-NA) system plays a crucial role in optimizing performance in different information processing domains and changes in its function plays an important role in age-related cognitive decline. Because pupil responses are linked to LC-NA activity, we investigated whether the expression of age-related differences in pupil responses depends on the interplay between age-related LC-NA decline and compensatory cognitive effort. To this aim, we measured pupil responses during two tasks differing substantially in their demands on compensatory cognitive effort: an n-back task and an auditory detection task. We found decreased pupil dilation of elderly participants during the high-demand n-back task, whereas no age differences were evident in the lower-demand auditory detection task. These findings are consistent with the idea that age-related differences in pupil responses emerge primarily under high cognitive demands, whereas under more modest task demands compensatory cognitive effort may partially offset the effects of age-related LC-NA decline. The pattern of our results also suggests that no general age-related differences can be shown for pupil responses accompanying cognitive processing. In contrast, age-related differences are determined by the exact nature of the involved tasks, and the emerging pattern of selective deficits can be informative regarding the underlying neural background. Keywords: pupil dilation, aging, cognitive control, n-back task, uncertainty, LC-NA system

Who Works from Home, and Who Enjoys It? Gender, Parenthood, and Affective Well-Being in Britain Before, During, and After the Covid-19 Pandemic

zhuofei lu, Matthew Henglong Luo, Yucheng He, Man-Yee Kan

Full text
Policy discourse in the United Kingdom presents flexible working as a remedy for work–family conflict, with mothers and carers as its principal beneficiaries. We ask who actually works from home, and who enjoys it, using UK Time Use Sur- vey diaries collected before (2014–2015), during (2020–2021), and after (2023) the Covid-19 pandemic. Uptake is measured as daily minutes of home-based paid work and as the share of paid work performed at home; affective experience is measured as episode-level enjoyment, modelled with two-way (person–diary) fixed effects among non-manual employees on weekday diaries. Working from home rose sharply during the pandemic and remained elevated in 2023, but mothers are not its principal users: in 2023 they record the lowest uptake of the four gender × parenthood groups. The affective evidence is period-dependent. Before the pandemic, women report lower enjoyment when working at home than on-site, whereas men report higher. In 2021, under lockdown, this pat- tern inverts: mothers show the largest positive home-working association of any group. By 2023 the lockdown pattern is gone: mothers, fathers, and childless women all report significantly lower enjoyment when working at home, and only childless men report higher. The one period in which mothers benefited was thus the period in which home-working was a universal mandate rather than a statutory entitlement. Flexible-working rights alone appear insufficient to deliver the affective benefits promised to mothers and carers; complementary care and workplace-norm interventions are required.

A qualitative analysis of the construction of responsible dog ownership in UK stakeholder policy and educational literature.

Carri Westgarth

Full text
A variety of campaign and policy messages in the field of dog ownership and canine welfare centre upon the fundamental concept of ‘responsible’ ownership. However, what this is perceived to entail is unstudied, and important if the design of interventions to promote it are to be effective. Therefore, this study aimed to explore the meaning and stipulations of ‘Responsible Dog Ownership’ (RDO) within UK stakeholder literature focused on education of dog owners and the animal professionals who manage them. Primary literature concerning RDO and canine codes of practice in the UK was collected (10 documents), along with a google search for UK hits for ‘responsible dog ownership’ (16 documents). An inductive thematic analysis was performed on the 26 documents. Owners were described as having a duty, requiring commitment, to provide personalised care for the dog, by seeking out the advice of qualified professionals. Actions were required to keep the dog under control, healthy (physically and behaviourally), in a suitable environment, with appropriate identification, and not allowing the dog to foul. Perceived responsibility of the owner extended to carefully considering where and how they source a dog. Clarity of current guidance on RDO is challenging due to the advice to do different things depending on the dog and context. Owners and the professionals they seek advice from are also failing to meet the clear stipulation to use reward-based training techniques and not use aversive, punishment-based methods. This study for the first time distils multiple varied guidance into one clear set of recommendations, including a suggested ‘Are you a responsible dog owner?’ checklist. This can be used as a framework by policymakers and educators to construct more detailed localised guidance around the duties and expected behaviours of dog owners in regard to UK law and wider societal perceptions.

Price Setting, Free Riding, and Equilibrium

Woongki Lee

Full text
We analyze strategic interaction among investors by distinguishing between price taking and price setting. The analysis shows that as price setting becomes more prevalent, equilibrium prices fall. Because this lower price benefits price takers as well as price setters, price taking can be understood as free riding on price setting. The gains from the lower price are distributed more heavily toward price takers. This asymmetry creates relative-comparison concerns, which can distort incentives and discourage price setting even when it would increase aggregate utility. We examine this problem through a pricing game and derive implications for strategic behavior and equilibrium outcomes.

Full text
The study was designed to investigate the ethical implications of behaviourally targeted political advertisements on young adults in Edo State, Nigeria, within the context of the rising trend of digital platforms and personal data use in political communications. The study was guided by Privacy and Surveillance Theory, which explains how personal data are gathered, tracked, and exploited, often against personal autonomy, privacy and democratic choice. The study used survey research as its design, and data were collected from young adults between the ages of 18 and 35 from Etsako West Local Government Area of Edo State, Nigeria. Using the Taro Yamane formula, the study established a sample size of 399, from which 396 valid copies of questionnaire were retrieved for analysis using SPSS. Nevertheless, there are ethical concerns associated with behaviourally targeted political advertising. The major ethical concern was related to the invasion of privacy by political actors. Therefore, the study concluded that behaviourally targeted political advertising has a weak impact on the opinions of young adults but has ethical concerns related to privacy and data misuse. It recommended data protection and digital campaign regulations and the promotion of transparent and credible political communication.

Effects of Cost Control Techniques on the Profitability of Listed Manufacturing Companies in Nigeria

Adriel Ekozin, Franklin Taiye Umaigba, Michael Jimoh Amedu, Doris Esohe Aiworo, Richard Usengbuwa, Ruth Abieyuwa Uhunamure, Endurance Izokun

Full text
The profitability of Nigerian manufacturing firms has come under sustained pressure from rising material, energy and overhead costs, currency volatility and intense competition, prompting renewed interest in cost control as a controllable lever of financial performance. This study examined the effect of cost control techniques on the profitability of listed manufacturing companies in Nigeria, disaggregating cost control into material cost control (MCC), labour cost control (LCC) and overhead cost control (OCC). Grounded in the Contingency Theory of Management Accounting and the Theory of Economies of Scale, the study adopted an ex-post facto design using a balanced panel of ten listed manufacturing firms over the ten-year period 2015-2024 (100 firm-year observations). Data extracted from audited financial statements were analysed using descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation and fixed-effects panel regression, with firm size and leverage as control variables and return on assets (ROA) and net profit margin (NPM) as measures of profitability. The three cost-control dimensions jointly explain approximately 57% of the variation in profitability (adjusted R² = 0.571; F = 27.4, p < .001). The results confirm that the systematic control of costs especially material and overhead costs is a significant driver of manufacturing profitability, corroborating recent Nigerian panel evidence. The study concluded that cost control techniques significantly affect the profitability of listed manufacturing companies in Nigeria over 2015-2024 and recommended prioritising material- and overhead-cost control, institutionalising budgetary control and standard costing and adopting contemporary tools such as activity-based costing and lean manufacturing.

Bieke Zaman, Priscilla Van Even, Jonas Rutgeerts

Full text
Digital transformation is reshaping how scientific knowledge is produced, circulated, and imagined. Yet this shift also amplifies concerns about epistemic narrowing, as digitally mediated, AI driven discourses consolidate dominant narratives and reinforce cultural imperialism. This chapter proposes arts‑based storytelling as a potential response. On the one hand, we discuss the potential of arts-based storytelling as a form of science communication different from the typical one-directional dissemination. On the other hand, we examine arts-based storytelling as a speculative practice of creative imagination and knowledge generation through fabulation. Measuring Meaning on Video at Scale, from classifier to theory interpreter: From Classifier to Theory Interpreter in AI-Based Humor Annotation

YAMAUCHI SHIMMEI

Full text
The rapid development of generative artificial intelligence (AI), particularly large language models (LLMs), has created new opportunities for measuring complex and culturally embedded phenomena. However, existing applications largely focus on textbased data and predefined classification tasks, leaving multimodal and interactional phenomena underexplored. This study proposes a framework for analyzing humor in video data by introducing humor events as the unit of analysis, defined as temporally bounded sequences linking an utterance, its multimodal delivery, and audience laughter. Using a large corpus of Saturday Night Live (SNL) clips, we extract and classify 5,690 humor events with a multimodal generative AI model. To examine how AI can annotate complex cultural phenomena, we compare three prompt strategies: a definition-based prompt, an interaction-oriented prompt, and a theory-informed prompt grounded in sociological theories of humor. Results show that the theory-informed prompt substantially improves performance, particularly for categories requiring contextual and multimodal interpretation. Beyond performance, the study demonstrates that prompt design systematically shapes how cultural phenomena are measured. While aggregate category distributions remain stable across prompts, label assignments at the event level vary in structured ways, indicating that prompts function as measurement frames rather than neutral instructions. The study makes three main contributions. First, it introduces an interactional and multimodal unit of analysis for video-based research, providing a concrete approach to operationalizing meaning in context. Second, it reconceptualizes generative AI not merely as a classifier but as a theory interpreter, capable of operationalizing sociological concepts when guided by theoretical prompts. Third, it shows that prompts themselves constitute measurement frameworks, suggesting that prompt design should be treated as an object of methodological scrutiny—potentially subject to evaluation and review in its own right. More broadly, the findings highlight that cultural phenomena are both structurally patterned and interpretively open, and that AI-based measurement inevitably reflects the analytical frames embedded in prompt design. Although annotating complex cultural phenomena remains challenging, the results suggest that generative AI offers a viable and scalable approach for video-based analysis when guided by explicit category definitions and theoretically informed prompts, providing a significant methodological advance over existing approaches

Fund Manager Beta in Tactical Asset Allocation

Woongki Lee

Full text
We show that realized returns are shaped by two opposing intertemporal forces. They rise with expected short-term returns but fall when risk subsequently increases. Successful timing therefore requires attention not only to favorable return expectations but also to future risk. This logic leads to a two-component specification of manager beta. One raises exposure when near-term opportunities are favorable, while the other lowers exposure when future risk is expected to rise. Market timing is thus represented as a tactical adjustment of portfolio exposure that seeks gains while limiting losses from subsequent risk increases.

Dual Exposure: Systematic and Systemic Risk

Full text
Systemic risk can be measured by the covariance between an institution’s return and the sector return. This allows systemic risk to be decomposed by decomposing the sector return. When the sector return is separated into a market-driven component and a market-orthogonal residual, systemic risk splits into systematic risk, reflecting exposure to the market-driven component, and net systemic risk, reflecting exposure to the residual. Full text
Chronic non-cancer pain is highly prevalent, and concerns about long-term opioid harms have increased interest in non-opioid therapies. Understanding which treatment attributes patients value is essential for shared decision-making. This systematic review synthesised discrete choice experiments (DCEs) to identify the treatment attributes patients prioritise when choosing non-opioid regimens for chronic non-cancer pain, and to appraise the methodological quality of the underlying studies.
